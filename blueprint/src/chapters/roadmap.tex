\chapter{Roadmap: manoeuvring targets}
\label{chap:roadmap}

What remains for the full master inequality is sufficiency against
\emph{manoeuvring} targets, plus the necessity of the window for drones that do not
fly steadily.

\begin{lemma}[Axis-angle]
\label{lem:axis-angle}
\lean{VisionTracking.exists_rotate_frame}
\uses{thm:rotation}
Two right-handed orthonormal frames differ by one Rodrigues rotation of angle at
most $\pi$. \emph{Stated in Lean; proof pending.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{remark}[Torque limits force smoothing]
The extrapolating formation strategy holds position to $O(\delta^3j)$, but its
thrust direction changes in jumps of $O(\delta j)$. Under bounded motor torque,
that means unbounded angular acceleration as $\delta\to0$. A manoeuvring formation
therefore needs a smoothing horizon $\tau$, set by the torque budget
$L f_{\mathrm{motor}}/J$, which trades position error $O(j\tau^3)$ for feasible
angular accelerations. Once that horizon is quantified, the master inequality
assembles from: Theorem~\ref{thm:speed} (directional limits),
Theorem~\ref{thm:steady-formation} (pointing in steady formation), the overhead
tilt-band results, and a lag margin fixed by $\tau$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Weaving drones]
Theorem~\ref{thm:window} constrains frames in which the drone holds its velocity.
A drone faster than the target could weave. Averaging the translational dynamics
(drag is convex) shows that its mean forward thrust is at least $c_Dv_T^2$, so the
tilt cannot be avoided on average. Turning this into a per-frame visibility bound
is open.
\end{remark}
