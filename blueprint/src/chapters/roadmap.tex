\chapter{Open directions}
\label{chap:roadmap}

\begin{remark}[What the numbers say: the theorem is proved, its constants are not yet useful]
\texttt{scripts/budget\_v1.py} evaluates the budget exactly as defined in Lean, for the
reference fixed-wing target and two quadrotors, with the smoothing family
$h^{(N)}$ and frame rates up to 500\,Hz. With the constants proved so far,
\texttt{MasterIneq} is \emph{not} satisfiable in the reference scenario. There are three
reasons.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Physics.} A single turn radius in every direction lets the target push
        over at almost $1g$ ($A_{\max}=a_\parallel+v_{\max}^2/R\approx g$). The
        copying quadrotor then needs vertical thrust down to $g-A_{\max}\approx0.26$\,m/s$^2$.
  \item \emph{Constants.} Triangle-inequality planner constants ($H_0\approx17$,
        $H_1\approx17\cdot$lag), the factor 2 in the normalisation bound, and $Z_{\mathrm{lo}}$
        (instead of $\norm T$) in the denominators.
  \item \emph{A design singularity.} Aligning the forward axis with straight down
        amplifies frame motion by $1/\sin(\text{tilt})$, which is large when the copying
        thrust is nearly vertical.
\end{enumerate}
All three are fixed in version 2 (Chapter~\ref{chap:yawfree}): sharp Abel-type planner
constants, a separate vertical manoeuvre limit, and the yaw-free down-looking camera
with a parallel-transported frame. \texttt{MasterIneq2} is satisfiable for targets up to
25--30\,m/s with jerk at most 2\,m/s$^3$. The reference 51\,m/s aircraft still needs
the hinged camera at high tilt, which is open.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Smoothing family and paper numbers]
A filter family $h^{(N)}$ with four frames of lag, $H_3=O(N^3)$ and $\mathrm{TV}=O(1/N)$
makes the torque-versus-lag trade-off explicit: the position error grows like
$j(N\delta)^3$ while the torque-relevant term shrinks like $1/N$. Evaluating the
budget for reference quadrotors and choosing $N$ gives the paper's numbers.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Weaving drones]
The steady-chase window constrains frames in which the drone holds its velocity. A
drone faster than the target could weave. Averaging the translational dynamics shows
that its mean forward thrust is at least about $c_Dv_T^2$, so the tilt cannot be
avoided on average. Turning this into a per-frame visibility bound is open.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Heading-locked formations]
For manoeuvring targets, only the overhead formation is proved sufficient. Holding
an offset that rotates with the target's heading (chasing from behind) costs extra
acceleration of order $d\omega_T^2$, and is the natural next sufficiency theorem.
\end{remark}
