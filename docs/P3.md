# P3: when can a quadrotor keep a fast fixed-wing target in view?

*Machine-checked in Lean 4 (no `sorry`, standard axioms only). Repository:
`AbdullahAlmanei/vision-tracking-lean`. Blueprint:
https://abdullahalmanei.github.io/vision-tracking-lean/blueprint/*

## The question

A quadrotor carries one camera, and it must keep a fixed-wing aircraft inside the image in
**every** frame. The camera has a 60° half field of view and sits on a one-axis hinge on
top of the airframe, looking 30–70° above the rotor plane. The aircraft is 3.5 m long with
a 2.5 m span and flies at up to 55 m/s, turning down to a 350 m radius. The goal (P3) is an
explicit inequality in the parameters that is sufficient for this, together with one that
is necessary, and an explicit account of the gap between them.

## The model

Everything is a full rigid-body quadrotor in discrete time with period δ.

**The quadrotor:**
- four motor thrusts, each between 0 and f_max, arranged in a plus layout with arm L and
  yaw coefficient k;
- an inertia tensor diag(J_x, J_y, J_z) and Euler's equation;
- quadratic drag c‖v‖v;
- a tilt limit, a body-rate limit and a speed envelope;
- attitude updated by exact rotations (Rodrigues' formula).

**The target:**
- speed band [v_min, v_max] and turn radius R;
- along-track acceleration a_tan;
- jerk, snap, climb angle and vertical acceleration limits.

**What counts as "in view":**
- the target is within detection range (8 pixels on its longer side), and
- its direction is within the half field of view of the camera's boresight.

**Strategies:**
- *position-only:* the drone sees only the target's past positions (from bearing and
  apparent size);
- *full information:* the drone also knows the target's future. Used only on the
  impossibility side, where it makes the result stronger.

## The results

### Sufficient side: `master_sufficient3`

If `MasterIneq3` holds and the planner reacts with a 4-frame delay, a position-only
strategy keeps every target that starts in steady flight in view in every frame.

The strategy (version 3, "underneath"):
- **Path:** the drone flies the filtered target path, offset 100 m straight down.
- **Thrust axis:** set by the planned translational motion.
- **Yaw:** locked to the target's sideways direction, which is well defined at every pitch.
- **Camera:** faces backwards. The hinge points it straight up, clamped to its range.
  Pitching nose-down for speed swings the camera up and over, so speed helps the camera.

`MasterIneq3` is a set of explicit conditions that must hold at every target speed s in
the band:
- **thrust:** positive vertical thrust, and the drone's bank angle below 90°;
- **frames:** consecutive attitudes close together, with tilt, body rate and hinge rate
  within limits;
- **motors:** the low- and high-thrust motor conditions, using per-axis roll and pitch
  torque and a separate yaw torque;
- **visibility:** camera offset + position error ≤ half field of view.

The bounds behind it come from:
- the planner's error constants and its snap bound;
- a component-wise analysis of body rates, with the rate written as δ⁻¹·ψ(a) for the
  antisymmetric part a of the frame-to-frame rotation;
- bounds on the yaw-locked frame;
- the geometry of the clamped hinge.

### Necessary side: `master_necessary3`

Suppose some strategy, even one with full information, keeps every steadily starting target
in view. Then `NecIneq3` holds: v_max ≤ dirSpeed(u) in every direction u the target may
fly, where dirSpeed is the drone's drag-limited top speed in that direction (motors, tilt
envelope, gravity).

The proof uses one target flying straight along u. It escapes **every drone trajectory**,
even one planned with knowledge of the target's whole future.

### The bracket: `p3_bracket`

`MasterIneq3` ⇒ every steadily starting target can be kept in view ⇒ `NecIneq3`.

## The numbers

These come from `scripts/budget_v3.py` and `scripts/gap_v3.py`, which mirror the Lean
definitions term by term. Setup: a 1.5 kg quadrotor with thrust/weight 6, c = 0.008, a
target band of 20–55 m/s, snap 15 m/s⁴ and vertical acceleration 1.5 m/s².

Largest target top speed allowed:

| Quadrotor | Dives | Necessary | Sufficient, jerk ≤ 1 | ≤ 2 | ≤ 3 | ≤ 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| base (k = 0.02, L = 18 cm) | ±5° | 85 m/s | 64 | 64 | 64 | 34 |
| base | ±10° | 84 m/s | 59 | 51 | 34 | 31 |
| k = 0.03, L = 22 cm | ±5° | 85 m/s | 65 | 65 | 65 | 64 |
| k = 0.03, L = 22 cm | ±10° | 84 m/s | 59 | 59 | 59 | 33 |

**Design rules** (each is the sufficient side; see the gap below):
- Top speed is capped by motors and drag (the necessary side) near 85 m/s for this airframe.
- Up to that cap, the sufficient side is limited by **torque at low total thrust**, mostly
  yaw torque. A larger yaw coefficient k and a longer arm L raise the jerk the target may
  have.
- Dives cost twice: drag reduces the vertical thrust left, and low thrust leaves the motors
  little room for torque.
- The camera is never the binding constraint: bank stays at or below 38° and camera offset
  at or below 40°, against a 60° half field of view.

## The gap, and what it means

The gap is torque and agility: target jerk against the drone's yaw and arm. We tried to
close it from both sides.

- **Smoother linear planning cannot help.** With a 4-frame delay, a position-only planner
  must extrapolate. Any linear filter that does this has gain Σ|h| ≥ about 2, so smoothing
  never brings planned jerk and snap below the target's own.
- **Jerk-limited nonlinear tracking does not help.** In prototypes against adversarial
  targets, lags reach 39–325 m and 13–35 m/s, because the target can reverse its
  acceleration just as the tracker catches up.
- **A sharper impossibility proof does not exist in this regime.** A target that keeps
  reversing its turns does force the thrust axis to rotate. But with 570 m of detection
  range and a 60° half field of view, a drone may respond over 7–30 s. The forced body rate
  is then 0.002–0.007 rad/s, against about 1 rad/s used by the design.

**Conclusion:** the jerk limits are a cost of the **tight formation** (exactly 100 m below,
with metre-level tracking), not of physics. The true wall is the motor/drag speed limit.
Closing the gap needs a looser formation that spends the slack in the camera and the range.

## Side results

- **Looking down from above** (`LookDown.lean`):
  - Seeing a target h below within range D forces tilt ≥ arcsin(h/D) + η_lo − α, whatever
    the yaw.
  - A depression e is held forever behind a straight, level target iff
    e ≤ chaseTilt(v) + α − η_lo.
  - A drone that pushes only along the track loses the target within an explicit time.
- **Earlier versions:**
  - Version 1, overhead with a hinged camera: proved, but never satisfiable at realistic
    numbers.
  - Version 2, yaw-free belly camera: up to about 30 m/s.

## Limitations

- **Thrust model:** motor thrust does not depend on airspeed. At 55 m/s, edgewise flow
  changes it.
- **No ground:** the model has no ground plane. Flying underneath needs the target to be at
  least 100 m plus a margin above the ground.
- **Target class:** necessity and sufficiency both use targets that start in steady flight.
- **Gap:** the gap in agility is explicit but large, as explained above.
